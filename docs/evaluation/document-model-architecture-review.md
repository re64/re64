# Document model: architecture assessment

This is an evaluation, not an adopted model design. It uses [purpose](../01-purpose.md), [architecture](../02-architecture.md), and the [implementation inventory](../document-model-report.md), read in that order. Architecture section numbers below refer to `02-architecture.md`; inventory section numbers refer to the report pinned to `f5b2720b38075edc96d851d42d73ae7afc277a13`. The inventory supplies sufficient structural evidence for this assessment; no additional implementation inspection was needed. Migration, backward compatibility, synchronization, complete schemas, and API design are outside scope.

The existing model has useful foundations: project-local identities, content-addressed external bytes, independently recorded interpretations, reusable definitions, and a separation between declarations and computed analysis. Its central layer–target arrangement is insufficient for the intended architecture, however. The redesign must distinguish material, machine state, analytical choices, assertions, and retained observations more precisely. Extending the existing roots without changing those distinctions would preserve the principal limitations.

## Existing objects and proposed disposition

**Retain** means the object's basic role remains suitable, not that every field is sufficient. **Reshape** preserves a useful concept while changing its boundaries or relationships. **Replace** means its current organizing abstraction should give way to different concepts. **Remove** applies to obsolete representations, not to the knowledge they may contain. Requirements below concern meaning and behavior; they do not prescribe collection layout or a stored entity for every concept.

| Existing object or group | Disposition and architectural fit | Required changes; choices left open |
|---|---|---|
| Project, `meta`, catalogue identity, plain project/export | **Retain** the project as the investigation's home. Names and descriptions remain editable metadata; catalogue identity can remain external. The current projection covers only part of the intended shared record. | Assets, knowledge, articles, findings, discussion, and retained evidence must belong to or be reachable from the project (§§1, 5–7). An export of declarations alone must not be mistaken for preservation of external material. Root placement, export packaging, and whether project identity is repeated in the document are open. |
| `files`, binary blobs, inline bytes | **Reshape** file declarations into an asset-capable material model; **retain** the distinction between project-local identity and immutable external content. Duplicate names and shared content are compatible with the architecture. | Assets must be independently inspectable and reusable without loading memory (§1). Identify locations within whole files, disk material, and extracted or produced content; record source and transformation context. A D64 member selector alone cannot identify all unnamed/deleted material or explain an extraction. Inline patterns need explicit resulting material when used or retained. Separate blob, asset, extraction, and locator records versus combined representations remain open. |
| Byte-bearing layers, their resource kinds and placement properties | **Replace** the layer as the combined owner of bytes, placement, annotations, and visibility. PRG/raw/ROM distinctions remain useful format or source information. | Separate asset contents and load-address hints from placement into a machine state's storage. Preserve distinguishable overlapping/hidden material and origins through copying and writes (§§1–2). A ROM is material used with a platform, not merely a specially positioned listing layer. Loading recipes and placement relationships may retain layer-like structure; their exact representation is open. |
| Targets, ordered links, target entry points | **Replace** the target's combined arrangement/view role with explicit machine state and configuration concepts. Ordered loading relationships and reusable setups remain useful. | Machine states must accommodate partial knowledge and enough detail to continue execution, including underlying storage, registers, banking, devices, hardware, and media. Configuration selects platform, placements or changes, interpretations, and experiment conditions (§2). A modified examination must identify its resulting state without changing the retained starting machine state. Link identity must distinguish repeated placements where needed. Entry selections belong to the examination; a function designation can also be knowledge. Named configurations, unnamed queries, machine state construction recipes, and shared implementation structures are choices; a list of shadowing layers alone is insufficient. |
| Root claims, loaded claim frames and interpretations | **Reshape** the central claim concept. Independent IDs, overlapping assertions, and descriptions fit; mandatory `at`, layer/target framing, and the narrow flat assertion vocabulary do not. | Represent a subject and assertion, including non-address subjects, relationships, function designations, historical readings, and conditional knowledge reusable across assets/machine states (§4). Distinguish applicability, assumed applicability, truth/dispute, and selection for analysis. Record contribution provenance even before evidence exists (§5). An asset-associated claim must be able to describe behavior elsewhere. Withdrawal and correction must remain inspectable. Structured assertion variants, predicate languages, applicability rules, and stored versus derived status are open. |
| Layer-owned comments | **Reshape** location notes and listing commentary. Their text and presentation ordering remain useful; absolute addresses owned by relocatable layers are an unsuitable universal anchor. | Anchor notes to the material, location, or examination they actually concern; relocation must not silently move their meaning (§§1, 4, 8). An intended assertion should be expressible as a claim, while free commentary need not become one. Separate note records, claim-associated prose, and view-local placement hints are possible representations. |
| Legacy labels, regions, symbol layers, nested bindings and import-only aliases | **Remove** compatibility-only representations. Naming, region interpretation, byte-independent platform knowledge, and bindings remain required capabilities. | Express their meanings through the redesigned claims, definitions, references, and configuration. No migration requirement justifies retaining parallel authoritative forms. Platform knowledge must remain expressible even without ROM bytes (§2); deleting a symbol-container abstraction must not delete that capability. |
| `primaryLabels` | **Reshape** the display preference. Choosing a displayed label without deciding truth is explicitly appropriate (§4). | A single project-wide numeric address cannot distinguish builds, storage, or relocated instances. Resolve preferences in the relevant examination and subject context. Preferences must not suppress alternative assertions. Whether preferences are personal, shared, saved with a view, or computed is open; an independent durable preference entity is unnecessary unless chosen. |
| `constantUses`, `labelUses`, their framed-site keys | **Replace** the two narrow operand-binding structures with a broader binding representation. The distinction between referring use and referent remains valuable. | Cover assets, locations, labels, constants, types, records, and fields; support non-address identifiers and shared-index relationships across tables (§4). Distinguish configured choices, asserted bindings, and inferred bindings. Site identity must accommodate the examination, data fields, and alternate instruction readings rather than just a numeric operand position. A unified binding type versus several explicit variants is open. Derived cross-reference indexes remain derived. |
| Constants, types, fields, parsed layouts | **Retain** reusable definitions and parent-owned field identities; **reshape** their use and historical references. Explicit record size, nested records, arrays, bit fields, pointers, and holes already serve §4 well. | Permit exploratory record interpretation without requiring a claim of correctness. Refer to records and fields in the context of material and the examined definition. Keep dependent uses discoverable and preserve the definition used by earlier evidence. Current definitions may evolve; retained results must not acquire the new meaning retroactively. Grammar versus structured types, numeric value domains, version storage, and whether record instances need durable IDs are open. Derived paths and decoded values need not become entities. |
| Decoders and compiled decoder cache | **Reshape** reusable decoder definitions; **retain** compilation as derived runtime state. | Identify additional inputs needed by a custom format, and retain the actual source/definition, parameters, inputs, output, and limits when its result is saved (§§4–5). Source text remains a valid definition representation. Explicit input declarations versus another inspectable contract are open. A compiled function or worker is not durable project knowledge. |
| Scenarios and steps | **Reshape** the repeatable execution specification. Existing inputs, state changes, bounded execution, checks, and capture requests provide a useful base. Step identities already support references. | Express reactive conditions and actions involving time, instructions, accesses, state, media, and deliberate interventions (§3). An execution must resolve the scenario together with its initial machine state and configuration; the scenario need not own those inputs permanently. Retained runs must identify the specification actually executed despite later step edits. Step containment, instruction vocabulary, and embedding versus referencing reusable conditions remain open. |
| Stored captures, transient capture outputs, machine runs, checkpoint cache | **Reshape** observation retention and run context; **replace** the cache-only checkpoint boundary as the sole representation of reusable captured state. | A capture is an observation; a checkpoint is a machine state usable for analysis or another run (§3). Retained captures need their actual producing run, state, inputs, interventions, and relevant context, not only mutable scenario/step IDs (§5). Runs and results can remain transient until retained, but retention must survive cache loss. Access traces must identify resolved storage/device accesses, including fetches. Dedicated run records versus contextual retained-result records, and checkpoint serialization or delta storage, are open. |
| Evidence, including `supports`, `refutes`, `retires` | **Reshape** evidence associations. Independent accounts supporting or questioning a shared claim fit the architecture; author/method/note are useful beginnings. | Reference source material, external sources, reasoning, comparisons, and retained analytical results as well as captures (§5). Explain relevance to the particular assertion; storing a result alone does not establish it as evidence (§8). Preserve what claim and definitions were examined through corrections. Withdrawal is a claim lifecycle decision, not itself a refutation; it may still be represented by an event rather than a status field. Whether evidence and relation notes are independent records is open. |
| Chat and participant records; external users and session history | **Retain** durable conversation and attributable contributors; **reshape** the mixture of contribution identity, participation history, and current liveness. | Messages must be able to reference project material, and the record must distinguish people/agents and identify contributions (§7). Session identities are useful attribution context but are not the identities of claims or evidence. Live `online` state, awareness, and leases remain runtime facts even if attendance events are retained. External identity catalogues, structured message links versus resolvable textual references, and the durable membership boundary are open. |
| Changes, tags, history and storage snapshots | **Retain** their durable recordkeeping role, subject to broader reference requirements. These are not all reconstructible from the current project projection. | Earlier reasoning and the examined state must remain understandable and resolvable (§§5, 7). Retained deleted storage alone does not establish those domain relationships. Tags may name states without becoming machine states; a storage snapshot is not a machine checkpoint. How revisions and history provide stable historical references is open. No synchronization mechanism is evaluated here. |
| Loaded project, memory map, claim/name indexes, analysis, routines/effects, rows and reports | **Reshape** contextual derivation; **retain** these primarily as computed results, not duplicate authoritative entities. Function designation and discovered function body are distinct. | Analysis must work directly on assets or on explicit state/configuration and selected claims (§§1–4, 8). Machine locations require access-sensitive mapping, rather than one active byte owner per absolute address. Instruction readings may overlap and functions/blocks may cross assets or occupy disjoint ranges. Preserve partial-state assumptions, unsupported behavior and unresolved accesses. Current-view results can be regenerated; retained historical results require their original inputs and output. Physical cache and index representations remain open. |
| Host ROMs/platform definitions, catalogue indexes, process and UI objects | **Reshape** platform input identification; **retain** the other boundary distinctions where useful. Platform code/resources and ROM blobs can remain external durable inputs. Rooms, stores, replica wrappers, callers, browser state, upload tickets, caches, awareness, and live emulators remain temporary runtime structures. | Configuration must identify the selected platform/resources; retained examinations must record the relevant versions/contents and defaults (§§2, 5). ROM knowledge uses the same descriptive facilities as project knowledge and remains usable without ROM bytes. Content lookup indexes and exports remain indexes/projections; the legacy blob-name compatibility index can be removed. View selection need not become shared project state. Exact packaging, caching, and material retention mechanisms are open. |

## Capabilities not covered by the current inventory

The preceding changes account for much of the gap. The following requirements need explicit model treatment beyond renaming existing objects:

1. **Material and machine-location references.** Distinguish an asset-relative location, a placement occurrence, underlying storage, and an access interpreted under machine state. Preserve extraction/transformation and later-write provenance. These are reference and lineage capabilities, not a requirement to store an entity for every byte or access.

2. **Machine states and resolved examinations.** Retain partial state, including unknown versus defaulted values, and full resumable state when available. Resolve configuration changes consistently across tools, preserving the original machine state. A temporary alternative can have identifiable inputs without acquiring a named durable machine state. A checkpoint becomes durable state when retained; a screenshot cannot substitute for it.

3. **General retained results and their dependency context.** Beyond scenario captures, retain comparisons, searches, queries, scripts, visualizations, static analyses, and investigator-produced results. Preserve examined material/state, operation and parameters, definitions, selected claims and assumptions, output, and reproduction limits. External output blobs remain durable material; their metadata and relevance can live in the project. Known dependencies must support impact review after corrections. Neither a program-only hash nor a mutable scenario ID describes all these inputs.

4. **Knowledge beyond byte annotations.** Support conditional and cross-context claims, provenance before evidence, recorded disagreements, and discoverable open questions. Separate an assertion's truth from its applicability and an investigator's decision to assume it. Record interpretation and field navigation must also work before an assertion is made. A question or disagreement might be a claim-related record, finding, or referenced discussion; the architecture does not demand one root per concept.

5. **Finds log and articles.** Findings need explanations of significance and annotated references to claims, material, results, definitions, discussion, and other findings (§6). They may start as leads. Articles need retained authored work and supporting material, including externally authored media and demonstrations. A finds log can be a collection/view; articles can be external artifacts with project references. Neither a built-in editor nor a stored article block tree follows from the architecture.

6. **Investigative coverage and hygiene.** Reports must expose decoded material, interpretation gaps, holes, competing assertions, broken references, and questionable applicability across selected contexts (§5). These are derived reports unless deliberately retained. The model must supply the distinctions they inspect: broad `data` coverage is not understanding, decoded code is not explained purpose, and a display preference is not a resolved dispute. Manually recorded issues complement mechanical detection.

## Consequential questions left for model design

These questions concern representations and semantic precision within the intended architecture; they are not reasons to preserve the old model.

| Question | Why the answer matters |
|---|---|
| **What exactly does each kind of location reference identify?** | A table loaded twice can share an asset-relative claim while having different placement-specific notes. At C64 `$A000`, a ROM read and a write to underlying RAM can concern different storage. A single layer ID or absolute address cannot answer both questions. Choose how material, placement, storage and access context compose, including range and field references. |
| **How are lineage and historical state retained at useful granularity?** | A decruncher produces bytes derived from compressed input and machine execution; a later write modifies only part of that result. Retaining just the final RAM hash loses the origins; retaining every execution event is not mandated. Decide what provenance supports tracing those bytes and how checkpoints identify complete state, unknown portions, defaults, devices, and media. |
| **How do claim subjects and conditions span reuse without asserting accidental equivalence?** | “This routine updates the collision state” may apply to both builds, while a claim about the new code at `$C000` applies only to the fix. A routine belonging to one binary may access ROM elsewhere. Decide how subjects correspond across material and states, how conditions are expressed, and how assumed, established, disputed, or unresolved applicability is recorded. |
| **What survives an edit as the same object, and what historical version does evidence refer to?** | Moving `ZoneRecord.name` from offset `$A0` changes what a field use reads even if its field ID survives. Changing a claim from “GOATS is required” to “OATS suffices” must not make old supporting evidence silently support the correction. Stable logical identities are useful; choose version, snapshot, or retained-context references that preserve the exact assertion and definition examined, including deletion behavior. |
| **What identifies a use, a function, or an exploratory record?** | The `BIT $02A9` and `LDA #$02` readings overlap but give the same bytes different roles. A function designation persists while analysis discovers different disjoint bodies under different incoming states. An investigator can inspect `zoneTable[0].name` without asserting the record interpretation. Decide when contextual references suffice and when durable identity is needed; do not equate a function with its current body or require a claim for every record view. |
| **How are assertion, configured choice, and inference represented without accidental promotion?** | One examination may force a predicate or select a tentative table-to-image binding. Its output must carry that assumption; displaying the resulting image does not establish the binding as true. Decide how selected claim versions, definition choices, applicability assumptions, and interventions are assembled into an explicit examination and carried into retained results. |
| **How are result retention, evidence relevance, and correction dependencies connected?** | A screenshot produced with substitute BASIC ROM bytes remains an observation of that experiment. A new ROM or revised scenario must not change its stated conditions, and the screenshot becomes support for a gameplay claim only through an explanation of relevance. Decide the retained-context boundary and which known dependencies link results, claims, findings, and articles for review. Some dependency links can be computed; semantic dependencies may need participant input. |
| **Which reference failures remain inspectable, and what preserves cited material?** | Deleting a type or extracted asset should not turn an article's cited old analysis into an unexplained literal or an untraceable image. Conversely, an unavailable ROM need not prevent reading ROM descriptions. Decide how missing current inputs, superseded definitions, withdrawn claims, and retained historical references are represented and reported. One silent fallback or universal deletion policy is insufficient. |

No concrete contradiction in the architecture, or essential capability that it cannot express, emerged from this assessment. The consequential work is to choose references, ownership boundaries, historical context, and retention semantics that realize it. Storage outside the document is compatible with that direction when durable material remains identifiable and retained; derivation or runtime caching alone is insufficient when the project intends to preserve the result.
